Beyond the Ordinary · visual direction, round 2

Three ways into the quantum world

Each direction shows the homepage and the double-slit exhibit. Everything that moves is calculated from the real physics, and every direction uses the same experiment so you can compare like with like. The sliders, switches and buttons inside the exhibit mockups work.

Mockups are desktop layouts, scaled to fit your window.

On a phone the mockups are small. A larger screen shows them properly.

Aurora Observatory

You step out under a night sky that is quietly doing physics. Calm, vast and celestial, with light as the main character.

How it feels
Slow, drifting and quiet, like a planetarium after the lights go down. Big italic serif headlines give it a literary sense of wonder.
How the physics shows up
As glowing light. Brightness shows where the wave is strong, so where particles are likely to land. The aurora on the homepage is a live two-source interference pattern.
Type
Cormorant Garamond for headlines, Figtree for reading. Warm amber is kept for your own predictions.
Watch out for
Space imagery can slide into wallpaper. Stars stay sparse, and the experiment is always the brightest thing on screen.

Try: move your cursor across the homepage. It shifts the phase between the two hidden sources, and the curtains of light slide sideways, as real fringes do.

Try: slide toward red light and the stripes spread apart. Move the slits closer and they spread too. Switch on the detector and the stripes disappear.

Night Tide

A dark, bioluminescent sea where every touch makes a wave. The most tactile and playful of the three.

How it feels
Alive and responsive. The world reacts to your hand right away, which invites play before any reading.
How the physics shows up
As moving waves. Brightness shows the wave’s value at each moment as it ripples, so you can watch crests and troughs cancel. The homepage water obeys the wave equation.
Type
Bricolage Grotesque for headlines (warm, a little quirky). Atkinson Hyperlegible for reading, a typeface designed for people with low vision.
Watch out for
Water is a metaphor. Quantum waves are not water waves, and the site has to say so plainly. This is also the least otherworldly of the three.

Try: sweep your cursor across the water. Where two sets of ripples cross they add up or cancel out, and the room names bob on the actual wave height.

Try: turn on the detector and watch the ripple pattern lose its stripes. Then clear the screen to see the particles build up again, one by one.

Phase Light

A dark, quiet hall where colour is never decoration. Wherever you see colour, it means phase.

How it feels
Precise and architectural, like a modern gallery with the lights low. The physics supplies all the colour, so it hits harder.
How the physics shows up
As the full wave. Hue shows phase (where the wave is in its cycle) and brightness shows probability. The homepage disc is a real quantum state changing over time.
Type
Syne for headlines (wide and distinctive), Instrument Sans for reading, JetBrains Mono for live numbers.
Watch out for
The most rigorous direction, but the coolest emotionally. The rainbow phase wheel needs gentle teaching and a second cue for colour-blind visitors.

Try: just watch the disc. It shows a particle in a bowl-shaped trap, in a superposition of two states. The pattern turns because the two states evolve at different rates.

Try: move the separation slider. Its colour matches the d in the equation, so you can see which number you are changing. Turn on the detector and the colours stop lining up across the two paths.

What stays the same in every direction

  • Every room follows Wonder → Predict → Experiment → Explain → Go deeper, and warm amber always marks your prediction.
  • Phase-as-colour is the shared visual language. In A and B it first appears in the Superposition room, where phase starts to matter. In C it appears everywhere from the start.
  • Every simulation carries a short note saying what is simplified and what is calculated at true scale.
  • Every animation can be paused, and the reduced-motion setting is respected. All controls work from the keyboard.
  • Directions can be mixed. For example, A’s atmosphere with C’s maths panel and phase colour.
